Who's behind this

About VettID

VettID is built and maintained by Al Liebl, its founder and architect.

The short version

I've worked in systems management, identity, physical security, big data, and cloud security since 1997 — which I've just realized is nearly thirty years, a fact I'm choosing to take gracefully. Along the way that's included time as an industry analyst at Meta Group (I left when Gartner acquired it, so no Gartner badge to claim), and nearly three decades of watching the same pattern repeat: security tools built for experts, and ordinary people left to fend for themselves.

VettID is my answer to that pattern. It's a non-custodial identity and communications platform built on Zero-Knowledge Trust: you hold your own keys, and there is no inbound path to your vault — not for attackers, not for the platform, not for me.

If you want the résumé version, it's on LinkedIn — fair warning: unlike this site, that link comes with tracking. Their house, their rules.

Where things stand

I'll be straightforward about the current state, because straightforwardness is the whole point:

  • VettID is in active development. The architecture is designed, specified, and being implemented in the open at github.com/vettid.
  • The legal entity for VettID is in formation — a short-term situation that this page will be updated to reflect.
  • VettID follows security and containment principles similar to those pioneered in the patent portfolio of Proteus Converged Systems, whose work has been cited as prior art by companies including Intel and PayPal. VettID does not currently use Proteus patents.
  • I work full time in the security industry and build VettID in my personal time.

What I believe

I believe technology is a gift from God — one that can genuinely make people's lives better when it's built with care, and one that harms people when it's built to extract from them. You don't have to share that belief to use VettID, and nothing about the platform asks you to. But it's why I build the way I do:

The user is sovereign, the architecture proves it, and no one — including me — gets a copy of your keys.

If you want the longer argument for why VettID exists, read Why VettID. If you want to see the work, it's on GitHub. If you want to talk, contact me.
